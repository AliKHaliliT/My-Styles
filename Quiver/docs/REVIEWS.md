# The rules for review records

The rulebook's rules for review records and screening logs. Part of the rulebook, [CONVENTIONS.md](CONVENTIONS.md), and frozen with it: owned by the style, changed only inside the template, never edited in a project built from it. Read before running or recording a pass.

## Rules for review records

A review record is the account of one pass over the literature: which question it served and what slice of it, what it searched and within what bounds, how it read, what it found, and what it changed. Records live in the `reviews/` folder under `docs/`, named `YYYY-MM-DD-short-kebab-title.md`, and follow this template: a `Date:` line, then the sections **Slice**, **Boundary**, **Method**, **Stages**, **Found**, **Changed**, and **Left out**, in that order.

- **Slice.** The question the pass served, named by number as `question N`, the part of it, and either the words `First pass` or a link to the prior pass record it extends, with the date it extends from, written as `from YYYY-MM-DD` and set before the day the prior pass ran by the lag of the index it searches, because a work dated before that day and indexed after it falls between two passes that meet exactly; the audit holds that the date stands before the prior pass's Date line, from the rule's arrival on, and the size of the overlap stays with review.
- **Boundary.** The catalogs, venues, databases and date range covered, enumerably; the eligibility criteria the screening applied, written before it ran, what a record needed to pass and what excluded it; each search string as run, in the database's own syntax, with the day it ran; and five closing lines. `Nearest review: [key]` names the review, survey or mapping study that already covers most of the slice, with the window it covered and what this pass adds, a newer window, a different question, a different index or a different method, or `Nearest review: none found` says that none stands near the slice in the indexes searched; the audit holds the line's presence and shape from the rule's arrival on, and whether the review named is the nearest stays with review. `Seeds: N fixed, N indexed, N found` followed by the seeds' keys names the works the pass fixed as relevant before any string ran, from the question's decomposition, an earlier pass, a review's reference list or the owner and never from the string they test; fixed counts them, indexed counts those the searched indexes hold, found counts those the strings returned, the numbers never rise, and an indexed seed the strings missed is named with the word the string lacked and the corrected string as a new run. A seed is a relevant work, so the pass enters it and Found names it whatever the strings did. `Seeds: none` says the pass ran no string to test, as a scoping read over works chosen by hand or a snowball from them does. The audit holds the line's presence, the numbers never rising, the key count against the fixed count and every seed among the keys Found names, from the rule's arrival on; whether the seeds were fixed before the strings and found apart from them stays with review. `Screening:` links the pass's screening log in the `screening/` folder, the record of the decisions its screening settled on, or says `none` for a pass that screened nothing; the audit holds the line, that the log exists in that folder, and that the rows its abstract stage passed are the funnel's screened count, from the rule's arrival on. A `Recorded:` line may name, by root-anchored path, the raw exports the searches returned, kept under `util_resources/searches/` and declared `linguist-generated`, and the audit holds that the tree holds what it names, or, where the ignore file keeps an export outside the tree, names it on every run as a check not run, present or absent on that machine. `Flow: retrieved N, screened N, entered N, read in full N` counts the funnel, the records the search returned or the seed set's size, those that passed the criteria, the works entered in the bibliography, which are the keys Found names, and those among them read in full, which are the keys Found marks full; the numbers never rise from left to right, the audit holds the last two to Found, and a pass with no search writes its seed count for the first two. `Completeness: exhausted` or `Completeness: judgment` closes the section. Exhausted means the completeness review ran against something published and found nothing more; judgment means the pass claims only what it read.
- **Method.** How the reading was done, named from the field's own vocabulary as the form the pass took, a scoping read, a rapid pass, a mapping pass, a multivocal pass, a systematic pass, a tertiary pass or a falsifying pass; the steps are the method files', `docs/METHOD.md` and `docs/FOLD.md`, and a step the pass skipped is skipped in writing in its stage line.
- **Stages.** One line per stage, `- <Stage>: ran by the owner, ...`, `- <Stage>: ran by the agent, ...` or `- <Stage>: collapsed, <reason>`, for Scouting, Enumeration, Checks, Completeness review, Fold, and Resolution. The owner is the person who answers for the inquiry and the agent is whatever ran on their behalf, roles and never names, so a reader knows which stages a person has judged and which a person has only read about; the audit holds the words over every pass added from the rule's arrival on, and whether they are true stays with review. Checks never collapse, their line answers for retraction, none retracted or the key and the day its publisher withdrew it, and a pass whose boundary is exhausted ran the completeness review; the audit holds the answer's presence from the rule's arrival on, and its truth stays with review.
- **Found.** The bibliography keys the pass consulted or added, each on its own line as `- [key] depth`, or, where the pass classified what it found, as one table whose first two cells are the key and its depth and whose further columns are the facets of the classification, one row per work, every key resolving and the depth one of three words. A classification is the finding of a mapping pass and lives in its record, dated, and a later pass over the slice re-issues the table whole with its additions, because a record is not edited. Full means the work itself was opened and read for the slice, whole where it is a paper and in its governing sections where it is a standard, a specification or a manual. Abstract means an abstract, a summary, a snippet or a page a search engine served was read and the work was not opened. Secondary means the work was known through another work and never opened. The audit holds the line's shape over every pass added from the rule's arrival on; whether the word is true stays with review.
- **Changed.** The conjectures opened or closed and the claims produced, by number, or the statement that the ledger did not move. A pass that found little says so here as its finding, with the funnel that measured it, because a thin literature is a result about the field and never a failure of the pass.
- **Left out.** What the boundary excluded and why, so the next pass knows where to extend, and by name each source the pass could not open, each search it prepared but could not run, written out whole, its string in the index's own syntax with its filters, its date range and the export to take, so a person runs it as written and the next pass enters it as run, and each verification it needs a person for, because those are the pass's work handed to someone with access, and a list that lives only in the writer's memory ends with the session.

A review record is immutable like every record and carries no `Status:` line, because a pass is not superseded; a later pass extends it. A `Cost:` line may close the record with what the pass spent, so the next collapse is decided from data.

## Rules for screening logs

A screening log is the record of every decision one reader's screening made for one pass, kept so the funnel's contents survive and not only its counts. The audit holds the shape below from the rule's arrival on; whether a decision was right stays with review.

- Logs live in the `screening/` folder under `docs/`, named `YYYY-MM-DD-short-kebab-title.md` after the pass they serve, and are immutable like every record.
- A log opens with a title, a `Date:` line and a `Pass:` line linking the pass's record, and holds one table headed `| Stage | Id | Work | Decision | Reason |`, one row per record per stage.
- The stage is `abstract` or `full`. The id is the record's identifier in the index that returned it, a DOI, an arXiv number or the index's own, never a link, because bare external links appear nowhere outside the bibliography. The work is the first author and the year.
- The decision is `included`, `excluded` or `unsure`, and unsure stands at the abstract stage alone. The reason of an exclusion is a criterion's label or a standard reason, and an inclusion's may stay empty.
- The log the Boundary links holds the decisions the pass settled on, a row the owner decided after a disagreement saying so in its reason, and its abstract-stage rows passed, included and unsure together, are the funnel's screened count.
- A second look's decisions are a log of their own, named after the pass with `-second-look` suffixed and linked from the Stages line that reports it, because two readers' decisions are two records and the disagreements between them are what the second look exists to show.
