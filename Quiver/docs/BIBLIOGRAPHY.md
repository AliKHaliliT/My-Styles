# Bibliography

Every work this inquiry consulted, as self-contained citations addressed by
key. A citation names the edition or dated version actually used and survives
the death of any locator riding on it. Prose cites keys, never bare links, and
this file is the one place an external locator may appear.

- **goldberg1991**: Goldberg, David. 1991. "What Every Computer Scientist
  Should Know About Floating-Point Arithmetic." ACM Computing Surveys, volume
  23, number 1, pages 5 to 48. Read for why error compounds under repeated
  rounding and accumulation.
- **ieee754-2019**: IEEE. 2019. IEEE Standard for Floating-Point Arithmetic.
  IEEE Std 754-2019, revision of IEEE 754-2008. Read for the definition of the
  roundTiesToEven attribute and its selection as the default rounding
  direction.
- **fowler2002**: Fowler, Martin. 2002. Patterns of Enterprise Application
  Architecture. Addison-Wesley, first edition. Read for the Money pattern and
  its treatment of rounding as an explicit design decision.
- **psf2026**: Python Software Foundation. 2026. "decimal, Decimal
  fixed-point and floating-point arithmetic." The Python Standard Library
  documentation for Python 3.14.7, as published at docs.python.org and read
  on 2026-09-29. Read for the rounding mode of the default context and the
  definitions of the two half modes.
- **oracle2023**: Oracle. 2023. "Class RoundingMode." Java SE 21 and JDK 21
  API Specification, package java.math, as published at docs.oracle.com and
  read on 2026-09-29. Read for the definitions of HALF_UP and HALF_EVEN and
  the notes beside them.
- **ecma402-2026**: Ecma International. 2026. ECMAScript Internationalization
  API Specification, ECMA-402, the living draft of the 2027 edition as read on
  2026-09-29. Read for the default rounding mode of the number formatter.
- **irs2025**: Internal Revenue Service. 2025. "Instructions for Form 1040
  (2025)." As published at irs.gov and read on 2026-09-29. Read for the rule
  on rounding to whole dollars and on adding amounts before rounding.
- **levy2011**: Levy, Daniel, Dongwon Lee, Haipeng (Allan) Chen, Robert J.
  Kauffman, and Mark Bergen. 2011. "Price Points and Price Rigidity." The
  Review of Economics and Statistics, volume 93, number 4, pages 1417 to 1431;
  read in the accepted manuscript deposited at EconStor, last revised November
  26, 2010. Read for the frequency distribution of the last digit and the last
  two digits of retail prices in scanner and Internet data.
- **holdershaw1997**: Holdershaw, Judith, Philip Gendall, and Ron Garland.
  1997. "The Widespread Use of Odd Pricing in the Retail Sector." Marketing
  Bulletin, volume 8, pages 53 to 58, Research Note 1; read in the copy the
  journal publishes at Massey University. Read for the end-digit distribution
  of 1,188 advertised prices in Palmerston North.
- **simmons2003**: Simmons, Lee C., and Robert M. Schindler. 2003. "Cultural
  Superstitions and the Price Endings Used in Chinese Advertising." Journal of
  International Marketing, volume 11, number 2, pages 101 to 111; read in the
  copy deposited in the Nanyang Technological University repository. Read for
  the distribution of rightmost salient ending digits over 499 advertised
  prices.
- **schindler1997**: Schindler, Robert M., and Patrick N. Kirby. 1997.
  "Patterns of Rightmost Digits Used in Advertised Prices: Implications for
  Nine-Ending Effects." Journal of Consumer Research, volume 24, number 2,
  pages 192 to 201. Read at abstract depth on 2026-09-30, for its finding that
  the digits 0, 5 and 9 are overrepresented among advertised prices.
- **stiving1997**: Stiving, Mark, and Russell S. Winer. 1997. "An Empirical
  Analysis of Price Endings with Scanner Data." Journal of Consumer Research,
  volume 24, number 1, pages 57 to 67. Read at abstract depth on 2026-09-30,
  for its scanner-data account of the preponderance of prices ending in 9.
- **mace2012**: Macé, Sandrine. 2012. "The Impact and Determinants of
  Nine-Ending Pricing in Grocery Retailing." Journal of Retailing, volume 88,
  number 1, pages 115 to 130, online in 2011. Read at abstract depth on
  2026-09-30, for its account of how often grocery prices end in nine.
- **strulovshlain2022**: Strulov-Shlain, Avner. 2022. "More Than a Penny's
  Worth: Left-Digit Bias and Firm Pricing." The Review of Economic Studies,
  volume 90, number 5, pages 2612 to 2645, online in 2022. Read at abstract
  depth on 2026-09-30, for its scanner-data account of 99-ending prices across
  3,500 products.
- **bray2006**: Bray, Jeffery, and Christine Harris. 2006. "The Effect of
  9-Ending Prices on Retail Sales: A Quantitative UK Based Field Study."
  Journal of Marketing Management, volume 22, numbers 5 and 6, pages 601 to
  617. Read at abstract depth on 2026-09-30, for the survey figure it cites
  that around 64 percent of retail shelf prices end in 9.
- **nguyen2007**: Nguyen, Adam, Roger M. Heeler, and Zinaida Taran. 2007.
  "High-low context cultures and price-ending practices." Journal of Product
  and Brand Management, volume 16, number 3, pages 206 to 214. Read at
  abstract depth on 2026-09-30, for its account of odd and even ending
  practices across countries.
