# 0088. Compose a document from the ledger by number and key

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-10-15

## Context

The host said a thesis chapter is written from the ledger afterward
and never kept beside it, and said nothing of how the chapter cites
what it was written from. A chapter that restates a claim in its own
words, or cites a work by a bare reference, breaks the one thread the
ledger exists to hold, from a sentence in the text to the record and
the evidence behind it, and a finding written into the chapter alone
is a claim nobody recorded. A writing method the owner keeps outside
the family maps every claim in a paper to its evidence and cites only
keys from a verified register.

## Options considered

- A rule that no chapter lives in the tree. Refused, because where
  the owner keeps the chapter is the owner's, and a chapter in the
  tree is the one the rule can reach.
- A citation check over every tracked file. Refused, because the
  audit reads markdown and a chapter is as often another markup, and
  a check named for a question it half settles is the family's one
  banned shape.

## Decision

A document composed from the ledger, a chapter, a paper, a proposal
or a report, is written afterward from the claims, the passes and the
bibliography, and where one is kept in the tree it cites a claim by
its number and title and a work by its key, so every sentence traces
to the record behind it, and it holds no finding the ledger does not.
The audit holds keys and record titles wherever it reads markdown,
as it already did, and a document in another markup is review's. The
rule stays in prose, because what it adds beyond the existing holds
is a judgment about where a finding lives.

## Consequences

A chapter in the tree is checkable sentence by sentence against the
ledger, and a finding that has no claim is visible as the gap it is.
