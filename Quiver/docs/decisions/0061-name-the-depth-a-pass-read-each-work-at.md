# 0061. Name the depth a pass read each work at

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-09-29

## Context

A pass record names the keys it consulted and a claim quotes the passage it
rests on, and neither says how much of the work was read. A passage can be
quoted from an abstract, a search engine's snippet or a summary as easily as
from the work itself, and the record reads the same either way. A literature
review run outside this template on 2026-09-28 turned out, when its author
asked, to have read most of its sources at abstract level and reported them
as read, and nothing in its records could have shown the difference. This
template's records could not have shown it either.

## Evidence

Measured on 2026-09-29 over this inquiry. Its one pass record says in prose
that its three works were read in full, and no rule held the sentence. Its
nine claims cite a key in their Claim sections and quote runs in their
Evidence sections, so no claim rested on a reading whose depth was unknown,
and the advisory decided here prints nothing over the demo as it stood. A
pass written for this record over four reference works read on 2026-09-29
carries the first depth words, and the selftest plants a pass at abstract
depth under a Supported claim and watches the advisory fire once, fall
silent when the claim names the depth among its threats, and fall silent
again when a later pass reads the work whole.

## Options considered

- Marking the bibliography entry instead of the pass. Refused, because depth
  belongs to a reading and not to a work, a later pass can deepen it, and
  the bibliography is living, so the history of when a work was read whole
  would be lost, while a pass record is dated and immutable and keeps it.
- A fourth word for a skim or a partial read. Refused, since a skim is an
  abstract-grade read for the purpose the word serves, which is whether the
  quoted passage was found in the work itself, and three words decide.
- Gating a Supported claim on full depth. Refused, because a claim may
  honestly rest on an abstract for a fact of existence, and its Threats
  section is where the claim says so; the audit advises and the claim
  answers.
- Binding the pass already landed. Refused; a merged record is not edited,
  so the check binds from the commit its scope sentence arrives in and the
  older pass keeps its prose.

## Decision

The Found section of a review record names each key on its own line
followed by one of three words. Full means the work itself was opened and
read for the slice, whole where it is a paper and in its governing sections
where it is a standard, a specification or a manual. Abstract means an
abstract, a summary, a snippet or a page a search engine served was read and
the work was not opened. Secondary means the work was known through another
work and never opened. The audit holds the vocabulary over every pass added
from the rule's arrival on, and it advises, on every run, each Supported
claim whose Evidence cites a key whose latest depth across the passes is
below full, naming the pass, until a later pass reads the work whole or the
claim's Threats cite the key. A key no pass names has no depth and the
advisory says nothing about it, because the missing pass is a finding of the
pass-discipline item and not of this rule. Under the ladder the line's shape
is gated and the depth itself is advised, since whether a work was read
whole is review's. The rulebook's Found bullet and Evidence bullet, the
guide's pass section, its advisory list and its pass-discipline item, the
map's pass paragraph and exemplar, and the invariants ledger name it.

## Consequences

A reader of a claim can tell a quoted abstract from a quoted work by opening
the pass that read it, and a claim resting on a shallow read prints at every
gate until someone reads the work or the claim admits the depth. The demo's
second pass shows the form with two more of its stages run than the first,
and its Left out names a source the tooling could not fetch, which a later
landing turns into a queue.
