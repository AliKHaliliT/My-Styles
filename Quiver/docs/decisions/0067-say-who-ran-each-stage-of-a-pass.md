# 0067. Say who ran each stage of a pass

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-09-30

## Context

A pass record says what each stage did and nothing about who did it. The
three passes this inquiry holds were run by an agent from end to end, the
screening decisions and the fold included, and the records read as if a
person had made them. A venue that asks how an assistant was used gets one
sentence, and that sentence is easy to write; the harder question is the
owner's own, which findings they have judged and which they have only read
about, and nothing in the tree answered it. The family's method study set
the division of labour in words, the owner owning the screening, the
extraction and the synthesis, and a division written in a memory is not a
fact in a record.

## Evidence

Counted on 2026-09-30. Twelve stage lines across the three passes say
ran, and none says by whom; every one was run by the agent. Under the rule
decided here the next pass writes the word on each stage, the audit holds
it over every pass added from the rule's arrival on, and the three passes
that landed before it keep their lines, as every rule that binds from its
arrival lets them.

## Options considered

- A sentence in the guide alone. Refused, because a word the audit can
  hold lands as a check under the ladder, and a sentence binds only a
  reader who already agrees with it.
- Names instead of roles. Refused, since a record names no person and the
  question the word answers is whether a person judged the stage, not
  which one.
- A word on collapsed stages too. Refused for now; a collapse names its
  reason, and who chose to collapse reopens if a collapse is ever found to
  have hidden a judgment nobody made.
- Leaving it out, and letting a project that needs disclosure write its
  own record. Refused, because the word costs nothing to write and the
  owner's question arrives after the passes were run, when the memory of
  who did what is gone.

## Decision

A stage that ran says who ran it, `ran by the owner` or `ran by the
agent`, the owner being the person who answers for the inquiry and the
agent whatever ran on their behalf, roles and never names. The audit holds
the words over every pass added from the rule's arrival on, refusing a
stage that ran without them, and whether they are true stays with review,
as the depth word does. The selftest plants a stage that ran with nobody
named. The rulebook's Stages bullet and freshness sentence, the guide's
pass section and pass-discipline item carry the rule, and the map's
exemplar now names the pass that carries the funnel line.

## Consequences

A reader of a pass sees which stages a person judged, and an owner
returning to an inquiry after a season sees which screening decisions and
which folds are still theirs to check before a chapter rests on them. The
word is self-reported, so it invites the same false comfort a depth word
does, and review reads behind it.
