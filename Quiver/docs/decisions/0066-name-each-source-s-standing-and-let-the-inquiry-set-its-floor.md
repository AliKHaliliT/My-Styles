# 0066. Name each source's standing and let the inquiry set its floor

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-09-30

## Context

The bibliography enters every work the inquiry consulted, a journal article
and a forum thread alike, because a source used and not recorded is the
dishonesty the rule prevents, and it said nothing about which kind each
entry was. A thesis may not rest a load-bearing claim on a blog post, an
industry project may, and a preprint is a different thing from the article
it later becomes. Nothing in the template could tell a reader which
standing a claim's evidence had, and a rule that fixed one standard for
every project would be wrong for half of them.

## Evidence

Counted on 2026-09-30 over this inquiry's bibliography. Sixteen entries,
ten of them reviewed articles, one a book, and five grey by the first tier,
a standard, a regulation's instructions, two runtimes' documentation and a
specification draft; no preprint. Two Supported claims rest on read
evidence, one on the documentation and the specification, the other on
three reviewed articles. Under a floor of reviewed the first would be
advised; under the floor this inquiry declares, the first grey tier, both
are silent, which is the inquiry's choice written where the check reads
it. The grey-literature tiering the family's methodology study priced is
the one used here.

## Options considered

- A ban on the lower tiers. Refused, because a vendor's specification or
  a repository's documentation is a legitimate source for a fact of
  existence in a thesis as in a paper, and the right rule is a floor per
  role, not a ban per tier.
- A floor fixed by the template. Refused, since which sources may carry a
  claim depends on the venue and the project, and a template that guessed
  would be wrong for the projects it did not have in mind.
- Marking the claim instead of the entry. Refused, because standing belongs
  to the work and is one fact per entry, while depth belongs to a reading
  and is already marked in the pass.
- A gate on the floor. Refused, as with depth, since a claim may honestly
  rest on a lower source and say so in its Threats.

## Decision

Every bibliography entry closes with one sentence naming its source's
standing, reviewed, preprint with the day it was last checked for a
published version, or grey by one of three tiers, and the file may declare
an evidence floor on one line, the lowest standing a Supported claim's
evidence may rest on, a preprint ranking with the first grey tier. The
audit holds every entry to a standing and the floor line to a legal one,
and it advises on every run for each Supported claim whose Evidence cites a
key below the floor, until the claim's Threats cite the key, and for each
preprint not checked in ninety days, until its date moves. With no floor
line no floor advisory fires, so a project that trusts a vendor's notes
declares nothing and hears nothing. The selftest plants an entry without a
standing, an illegal floor, a claim below the floor with and without the
key among its threats, and a preprint checked years ago and today. The
rulebook's bibliography rules, Evidence bullet and freshness sentence, the
guide's advisory list and records-discipline item, and the invariants
ledger carry the rule; the demo's entries carry their standings and its
floor.

## Consequences

A reader of a claim can see what kind of source it rests on without
leaving the bibliography, a thesis declares its floor once and the audit
holds it, and a preprint cannot sit uncited in its published form for a
season without saying so. The template guesses no floor on any project's
behalf.
