# 0057. Leave a merged record's defects standing

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-09-23

## Context

Record 0055 landed on 2026-09-23 with a clause-colon splice in its
evidence section, found after the merge. The rulebook banned that splice
before the record was written, so this is not a case of a new rule reaching
back, and the rulebook also said that a Status line and a link target are
the only edits an accepted record may receive, so the defect could not be
repaired. What the rulebook did not say is what becomes of such a defect,
and a child's agent that meets one in an inherited record had nothing to go
on but its own judgment, which is the one thing immutability exists to keep
out of a record.

## Evidence

Record 0050 refused to widen a record's legal edits to punctuation
when the em dash count was scoped, so the family had already decided the
repair half without deciding the standing half. Record 0053 moved
a check's anchor forward when the check widened, which is how the family
answers the other case, a new rule meeting older records, without ever
listing an exception. The treasury holds one erratum, a reviewer count
corrected in a study on 2026-08-24 on the owner's word, the only edit a
merged record has ever received in the family and an allowance written
nowhere; the treasury's ruling of this date renounces it.

## Options considered

- Repairing the splice. Refused, because the check that decides a legal edit
  would need a clause for punctuation, and because a record is evidence of
  its day, which nobody corrects, the style included.
- A note beside the record naming the defect. Refused, since a list of
  tolerated violations reads as permission and grows.
- Saying nothing and trusting review to know. Refused, because a child's
  agent reads the rulebook and not the reviewer's mind.

## Decision

The Records paragraph of the rulebook gains the sentence. A defect found in
a record after it merged stays, a wording, a figure or a mark of punctuation
alike. A later record supersedes it where the defect changes what was
decided, and nothing supersedes a wording, because a record is evidence of
its day and never a precedent for new writing; the law a new record is held
to is the rulebook's, not an older record's. Under the ladder the sentence
is prose, held in review, and it changes no check.

## Consequences

The splice in record 0055 stays. A defect found in a record from now on
gets a superseding record where it changed a decision and nothing where it
did not, and the effort that would have gone into repairing records goes
into the gate before the merge, where a defect can still be refused.
