# 0119. Name the judge of a stage and not its drafter

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-11-03

## Context

A pass record names who ran each stage, the owner or the agent, so a
reader knows which stages a person judged and may rest on without a
second look. The word was defined by the stage's running, and a stage
the agent drafted and the owner then judged item by item against the
source, every quote read on its page and every decision made by the
owner, fell between the two words. The question arose on the first pass
whose fold the owner judged in full, and neither word said whose stage
that was.

## Options considered

- Delete the word. Refused, because the stages a person judged are the
  ones a claim may rest on without a second look, and without the word
  every claim is one a reader must verify from the start.
- Name both, the drafter and the judge, on the line. Refused, because
  the line's question is whose judgment the stage carries, and a line
  that names both answers it twice; what prepared a draft is no more the
  record's business than the editor the text was typed in.
- Name the owner only where the owner worked from no draft. Refused,
  because it makes the word depend on how the work was done rather than
  on who decided it, and a judgment made against the source is the same
  judgment whatever was on the desk before it.

## Decision

A stage is the owner's when the owner decided every item in it against
the source, whatever prepared the draft, and the agent's otherwise. The
method's rigor paragraph and the rulebook's rule for the Stages section
carry the sentence. The audit holds the words as before, and whether
they are true stays with review.

## Consequences

A stage the owner judged item by item reads as the owner's and carries
the standing of a person's judgment, a stage the owner only read about
keeps the agent's word, and the passes already recorded stand as
written, since a record is never edited.
