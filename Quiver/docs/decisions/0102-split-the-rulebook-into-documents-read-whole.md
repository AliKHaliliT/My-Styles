# 0102. Split the rulebook into documents read whole

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-10-17

## Context

The rulebook ran from five thousand six hundred words in the seats to
eight thousand in the host, three times what a reader takes in whole,
because every rule the family learned about its documents had been
added to it as a section, and the guide's split had already shown that
a document read to its end has to be a document a reader can hold. The
rules a worker needs at one moment are a few sections. The species and
the spine bind when a document is created, the record rules when one is
written, the prose law whenever prose is, and the rest sat between them.

## Options considered

- Leave the rulebook whole and trust the reader to find the section.
  Refused, because the guide says the rulebook is read before writing
  any documentation, and a reader does not read eight thousand words for
  the three hundred that bind the change in hand.
- Cut by the budget alone, a line or word count each file must meet.
  Refused as the first move, because a cut made to a number lands
  wherever the number falls; the cut is made at the seams a reader
  already uses, and the budget then holds what the cut produced.

## Decision

The rulebook is read as a core and the documents it names, each flat
under `docs/`, frozen as the core is and registered in the index and the
spine. They are the rules for living documents, the rules for records
and the prose law in every seat, and in the host the rules for review
records and screening logs and the rules for the bibliography beside
them. Every section moved as it stood. The family audit holds each shared document
whole across the seats and carries each into the arrow, the audits' own
lists name the new files where they named the rulebook, and the prose
law's list of tells moves the vocabulary advisory's skip with it.

## Consequences

A reader takes each rule document in whole, the index says when each is
read, and the budget the family moves to next has documents to hold.
