# 0065. Count a pass's funnel in its boundary

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-09-30

## Context

A pass that searches a database returns hundreds of records and reads a
handful, and the Boundary named the databases, the terms and the dates
without saying how many records came back, how many passed the screening,
by what criteria, or how the search was actually phrased. A reader who
wanted to repeat the search or weigh its claim of judgment had the terms
in prose and nothing to count. The systematic-review tradition prices this
as study selection and the flow of records, a two-pass reduction with the
counts at each stage and the criteria written before screening, and the
family's own boundary-honesty item asks for the enumerable boundary a
completeness claim exhausted.

## Evidence

On 2026-09-30 the inquiry's third pass searched one index for works on the
endings of retail prices. The index returned 460 records, the pass
examined the 60 most cited, nine passed the criteria, and two open copies
were read in full. Written under the old bullet, the record would have
said which index and which terms and left the reader to guess that 460
had become two. The audit already holds the keys Found names and the
depth beside each, so the last two numbers of a funnel line can be held
to the record itself, and the first two are the pass's own account.

## Options considered

- A flow diagram in the record. Refused, because four numbers on one line
  carry what the diagram carries and a line is checkable while a picture
  is not.
- Counting only for passes that searched. Refused, since no tool can tell
  a search from a seed set, and a seed-set pass writing its seed count
  four times costs one line and keeps the check uniform.
- Holding retrieved and screened to something. Refused, because nothing
  in the tree knows what an index returned; those two numbers are the
  pass's word, and review reads them, while entered and read in full are
  held to Found.

## Decision

The Boundary states the eligibility criteria before the screening runs,
each search string as run in the database's own syntax with the day it
ran, and one line before its completeness line,
`Flow: retrieved N, screened N, entered N, read in full N`.
The numbers never rise from left to right, entered equals the keys Found
names, and read in full equals the keys Found marks full; the audit holds
all three over every pass added
from the rule's arrival on, and a pass with no search writes its seed
count for the first two. The selftest plants a pass without the line, one
whose funnel rises, one whose entered count disagrees with Found, and one
whose read-in-full count disagrees with the marks. The rulebook's
Boundary bullet and freshness sentence, the guide's pass section and its
pass-discipline item carry the rule, and the demo's third pass shows the
form.

## Consequences

A search can be repeated from its record, its reduction can be read at a
glance, and a claim resting on a pass can be weighed against how much of
the index the pass actually opened. The two passes that landed before
this rule keep their boundaries as written.
