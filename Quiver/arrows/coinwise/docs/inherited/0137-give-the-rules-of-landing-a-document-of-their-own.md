# 0137. Give the rules of landing a document of their own

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-11-24

## Context

The rules of conduct reached 1,949 of their 2,000 words of prose with
the rule on closing notes, and the next rule the style's owner asked
for, a fixed shape for a short summary, would carry them past the
budget, whose remedy is fission by subject. One rule, the rule on
landing, took 489 of those words, a quarter of the document, and it
covers one subject from a session's working tree to the run that lands
its change.

## Evidence

Counted on 2026-11-24 at a32dbd3 with the audit's own count of prose.
The rules of conduct held 1,949 words in thirteen rules, and the rule on
landing held 489 of them, while the next largest held 294. With the rule
moved out, the rules of conduct hold 1,473 words. No audit names the
rules of conduct in its code beyond the family audit's list of shared
texts, since a document under `docs/` is held by its row in the index,
its name and its budget wherever it sits.

## Options considered

- Raising the budget. Refused, because the budget measures what a reader
  takes in whole, and a longer document is read less carefully, not
  more.
- Moving the rules that speak to the owner into a document of their own.
  Refused, because they share their reasons with the rules on pausing
  and reading, which stay in the rules of conduct, while the rule on
  landing stands alone in its subject.
- Splitting the rule on landing into several rules as it moved. Refused,
  because a move that changes no word is one a reader can check against
  the old text, and reshaping it is a change of its own.

## Decision

The rule on landing moves word for word into `docs/LANDING.md`, the
rules of landing, in every seat, and leaves the rules of conduct. The
new document is read whole before any work, as the rule was where it
stood, because its first sentence, one working tree and one branch per
session, binds a session from its start. The guide registers it and
names it beside the rules of the tree and the rules of conduct, the
rulebook's table of documents lists it, the gate's item on the hard
rules and the introductions of the other two rule documents name it, the
adoption document recopies it at re-alignment, the host's README draws
it in its tree, and the family audit holds its four copies to one text
and the arrow's to the package seat's.

## Consequences

The rules of conduct have room for the rules the owner asks for next. A
project re-aligning to this commit recopies one more document, and its
worker reads one more document before any work, with no rule added or
removed.
