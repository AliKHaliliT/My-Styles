# How to use the research methodology vocabulary

Living guidance for reading study 0004's findings. Rewritten in place as the
practice sharpens, and never part of the record.

## What this vocabulary is for

It exists so that a question about how to find something out starts from the
field's own names rather than from whatever the person asking happens to
remember. Treat every entry as an option to weigh, never an obligation to
satisfy. The point is that an omission becomes a decision instead of an accident.

It is a reference work of 2,908 entries and it is not meant to be read through.
Read the family that covers your question, and reach into the others by name.

## Read the price, because the price is the finding

Every entry ends one of two ways, and the difference is the whole value of the
catalogue.

An entry that names something a person CHOOSES ends by naming what the choice
costs. That cost is what a reader deciding actually needs, and it is denominated
in whatever that kind of thing takes from you. A design costs units, time, and
assumptions about who can be assigned. An identification strategy costs an
assumption, and the entry names which one. A reporting guideline costs effort. A
code of conduct costs whatever it forbids that you might otherwise want.

An entry that names something that HAPPENS to an inquiry ends at definition. A
bias, a threat, a fallacy, a failure mode has no price, because nobody chooses
it. Those entries are written so a reader can RECOGNISE the thing in their own
work, which is the only use they have.

That split is a gradient across the whole subject, running from seven percent
priced in the bias catalogue to ninety-four percent in reporting guidelines. So
the family you are reading tells you what kind of reading to expect. Early
families are mostly free to know. Later ones are mostly choices with costs.

## The order carries an argument

The thirteen families run in the order an inquiry moves through them, and reading
a family before reaching into it is worth the time.

Foundations frames a question. Threats, biases and fallacies names what can
destroy the answer, and it sits second on purpose, because a reader should meet
the ways an inquiry fails before choosing among ways to run one. Causal
identification, the two design families and Measurement are how a study is set
up. Interpretive and case-based inquiry stands beside them at full parity, not
beneath them. Inference and analysis, and Computational and simulated evidence,
turn observation into claim. Synthesis and secondary evidence uses what other
people produced. Evaluation and implementation asks whether it worked in the
world. Conduct and integrity, and Reporting, communication and reproducibility,
cover doing and telling it honestly.

## Look a name up rather than browsing for it

Most of what this vocabulary holds is reachable under a name you already know.
The 2,908 entries carry roughly thirty thousand alternate names, which is where
the acronym, the other discipline's word, the eponymous form and the spelling
variant live. Search the file for the term you know, and you will usually land on
the entry that covers it even when your term is not the headword.

Where a merged entry folds a published list, the members are named inside the
entry and carried as alternates, so a single strategy, technique or criterion is
findable by its own name.

## Two things the vocabulary deliberately does not do

It does not resolve every name that two families both claim. Seven hundred and
seventy-one names appear in more than one family, and most of those are one thing
entered twice because two families were given overlapping headings. Where a name
genuinely carries two senses, both entries state their sense. Where it does not,
you may meet the same thing twice, and the study's resolution rulings record
which family is its canonical home.

It does not transcribe every enumeration. Where a source lists a hundred codes,
the vocabulary carries the category structure, the members that bear a decision,
and every code as an alternate name, and the exhaustive list stays in the source.

## What to distrust, stated plainly

One group of twenty-one entries on threats to a research synthesis rests on
unverified recall of a paywalled table. The names are canonical and their
arrangement is not confirmed. Check the source before relying on the grouping.

Entries built from held knowledge can carry a superseded edition of a document.
Several were caught and corrected, and others may remain. Where an entry names a
standard, a code or a guideline and the wording matters to you, check the current
edition.

## When to run the study again

Run it again if the applied gradient stops holding, if a whole naming culture is
missing, or if the newest region has settled. The generative-model conduct
vocabulary was the least settled part of the subject when this study ran, and it
was entered only where a named body or a named paper actually used a term. That
region will be the first to date.
