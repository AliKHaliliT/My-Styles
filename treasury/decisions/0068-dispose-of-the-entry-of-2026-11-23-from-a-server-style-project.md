# 0068. Dispose of the entry of 2026-11-23 from a server-style project

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-11-23

## Context

One project built from the server style, with a client from the client
style kept in a folder of it, holds one upstream entry, dated 2026-11-23
and filed as a defect, pinned at the family's 6c9473b, which the
family's history holds. It names three breaks of the timestamp fix the
family landed that same day that pass the server style's suite, found by
proving the suites the project took one change at a time. The style's
owner asked for the entry resolved and for the landing to wait for his
word. Tested at the pin, the entry was real. The family had proven that
fix and the rest of its batch by breaks made several points at a time,
and breaking each change one point at a time found eight more gaps of
the same kind, two in the same fix and six in the patterns the audits
hold.

## Decision

- Three breaks of the timestamp fix pass the server style's suite. Kept,
  in the entry's form, and widened. Each schema refuses a stamp without
  its offset in a case for each field, a case generates a revision
  through a copy of the migration environment and applies it, the
  column's time zone is held for a database that keeps offsets, and the
  environment's offline pass of the renderer, which nothing ran, leaves
  it. The server seat's 0141.
- Six parts of the audits' patterns held by no plant, four found in the
  follow-up and two as the new plants were proven. Each part now has a
  plant of its own. The package seat's 0134, the server seat's 0142, the
  client seat's 0134 and the host seat's 0129.

## Options considered

- Writing into the test rule that a change is broken one point at a
  time. Refused, because the rule already breaks a change against a
  closed list and counts each survivor as a gap, and the miss was in
  applying it. A sentence would add nothing a check holds.

## Consequences

The project deletes the entry at its next re-alignment and takes the
template's cases, which hold what its own copies hold. A change in the
family is proven by breaking each of its points on its own, a field, an
alternative of a pattern and a line that wires one part to another, as
the test rule already asks.
