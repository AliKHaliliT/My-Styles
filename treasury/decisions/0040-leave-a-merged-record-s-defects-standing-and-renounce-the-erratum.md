# 0040. Leave a merged record's defects standing and renounce the erratum

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-09-23

## Context

On 2026-09-23 four seat records landed with one clause-colon splice in
them, found after the merge, and the owner asked what the family does when
something new makes an earlier record wrong. Patching a record teaches every
child's agent that immutable means immutable until inconvenient. Leaving a
known defect and mentioning it beside the record reads as permission to
break the rule, and a list of tolerated exceptions in a mechanical check
grows without end. The question covers two cases that look alike and are
not.

In the first case a new rule arrives and older records break it. That is
already law. A rule binds from the day it arrives, a history-reading check
binds from the commit its scope sentence appeared in, and a check that
widens changes its sentence so its anchor moves forward. Rulings 0036 and
the list-marker repair of the same week are the two worked examples. A
record written before a rule is not a violation of it, and nobody lists it.

In the second case a record broke a rule that bound when it was written,
which is the splice. Ruling 0036 refused to widen the legal edits of a
record to punctuation, so the record could not be repaired, and yet no rule
said in words what becomes of such a defect. The style itself had once
acted otherwise. On 2026-08-24, commit a4f5c04 corrected the reviewer count
in study 0003's research record from six to five on the owner's word, an
allowance written nowhere.

## Options considered

- Patching the defect. Refused, because the check that decides a legal edit
  would need a clause for punctuation, which 0036 refused, and because the
  style's authority is over the law, not over the evidence of what it
  decided on a given day.
- A note beside the record, or a list of known defects. Refused, since a
  list of tolerated violations reads as permission and grows.
- An exception list in a mechanical check for the artifacts a new rule
  would redden. Refused, because a check binds from its arrival and moves
  its anchor when it widens, so no such list is ever needed.
- Keeping the erratum allowance, narrowed to figures. Refused, because a
  list of allowed edits is the exception list the owner was worried about,
  and it starts at one. A miscount gets a superseding record of two lines.

## Decision

Every rulebook's Records paragraph and the treasury's rules say it in
words. A defect found in a record after it merged stays, a wording, a
figure or a mark of punctuation alike. A later record supersedes it where
the defect changes what was decided, and nothing supersedes a wording,
because a record is evidence of its day and never a precedent for new
writing; the law a new record is held to is the rulebook's, not an older
record's. The erratum allowance is renounced. The one erratum stands as
history, since reverting it would be a second edit, and its commit message
is its record. The first case is restated here as the family's general
answer so that both cases are found in one place. A rule binds from its
arrival, and a check that widens moves its anchor.

## Consequences

The splice in the four records of 2026-09-23 stays where it is, in every
seat. A child's agent that meets a defective inherited record has a written
answer and nothing to improvise. The next defect found in a merged record
gets a superseding record or nothing, and the remedy for defects moves to
where it belongs, before the merge.
