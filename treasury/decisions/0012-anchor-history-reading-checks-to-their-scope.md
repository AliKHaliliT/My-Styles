# 0012. Anchor history-reading checks to their scope

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-09-04

## Context

The adopting doctoral repository sent a second finding, one entry, with the
family's records checked and named. Its gate had gone red after it
re-aligned to the record-folder rule, over twenty-eight edits made two days
earlier to documents that were living then and became records on the
reshape day. The record-immutability check dated its arrival by searching
history for its function's name, and that repository had written a check of
the same name before the family adopted the idea from it, so the widened
rule found the older arrival and judged commits that were legal when made.
The finding named the general hazard: a child that contributes a check is
the child most likely to be punished by it later, because its history
already carries the shape.

## Decision

Kept, with the anchor the finding proposed and the alternative it offered
refused. A check that reads history binds from the arrival of its own
scope, dated by the sentence that states the scope and found in the tree's
own history, never by a name, a date, or the README's alignment pin, which
names a template commit and bounds no range in a child's history. When the
scope changes, the sentence changes with it, so a widened rule never reaches
behind its own arrival. The rule enters every agent guide beside the one on
checks implying no more than they decide, every audit's immutability check
carries and searches for its scope sentence, and the host's selftest proves
the anchor is the commit that introduced it.

The same pass settled two neighbours of the finding. A Python module carries
no docstring, since none of the family's package modules does and a foreign
lint rule had produced two hundred; the code-level section says so and the
audit holds it. And re-alignment sweeps what retired rules produced, by the
finite diff of the child's configuration against the style's, since a
closed list of forbidden artifacts cannot be complete and would imply more
than it decides.

## Consequences

The adopting repository's gate can go green on a history that was legal
when written. The family gains a stated rule for every future check that
reads history, and a procedure for the leftovers a foreign rule leaves
behind, each leftover class met in practice becoming a named negative in
the checks.
