# 0061. Keep the qualifier that bounds a claim

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-09-16

## Context

The prose law cuts hedges, filler, and theater, and it is applied on every
landing to every tracked byte of living prose. Nothing in it distinguished a
hedge from a boundary. "No known remaining risks" and "no remaining risks"
differ by one word, and a pass that reads the word as a hedge and removes it
has turned a bounded claim into a false one, in the family's own voice, with
the law's approval. Treasury study 0005 found the distinction stated in the
published agent-skills ecosystem by a tool that strips filler from agent
replies and says, first, to keep qualifiers that change what a claim
promises, and the family disposition, treasury 0030, Take eight rules from
the skills study and refuse the rest, adopted it as one paragraph in the
prose section.

## Evidence

The prose section's rules on 2026-09-16 named hedge phrases as banned
characters' companions in the study method, listed the padding phrases to
cut, and required a claim to name its source, and the word qualifier
appeared nowhere in the rulebook. The cut for filler is the family's most
frequently applied editing rule, because it runs on every prose change, and
the family's records and reports carry bounded claims in every section, so
the two meet on every landing. The source tool states the rule as the first
of its principles and gives the same example.

## Options considered

- A list of protected qualifiers. Refused, because the list would be the
  vocabulary grep's mirror image, a set of words that are sometimes
  boundaries and sometimes hedges, and the difference is in the sentence,
  not the word.
- A check. Refused under the ladder, because whether a word bounds or hedges
  is what the sentence promises without it, and no tool reads a promise;
  the rule lands as prose, the third rung, with a test a reader can run in
  one reading.
- Leaving it to review. Refused, because review applies the law as written,
  and the law as written said to cut.

## Decision

The prose section gains one paragraph after the rule that a claim names its
source. It says that a qualifier that bounds a claim is not filler and
survives every cut, gives the "no known remaining risks" example, and states
the test, whether the sentence promises more without the word. The paragraph is byte-identical in
every rulebook and the arrow carries it. This rule lands as prose because no
check can read what a sentence promises.

## Consequences

A worker cutting filler now has a test for the word that looks like a hedge,
and a bounded claim in a record or a report keeps its boundary through every
later pass. Nothing mechanical changes; the vocabulary grep is unchanged and
still advisory.
