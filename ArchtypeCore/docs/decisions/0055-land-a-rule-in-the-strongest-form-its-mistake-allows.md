# 0055. Land a rule in the strongest form its mistake allows

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-09-16

## Context

Treasury study 0005 read the published agent-skills ecosystem against the
family's law and found one preference the family practises everywhere and
states nowhere. The ports that make a layering mistake a type error, the
import linter that refuses a dependency instead of a review catching it, the
family audit that holds a carried copy byte-identical instead of a sentence
asking for care, and the shape limits the linter gates instead of a paragraph
on readability all make a mistake impossible or caught before a reader is
asked to remember anything. The guide's two-tier rule states the bottom of
that ladder, a check that decides its question gates and one that cannot
advises, and nothing states the top. A worker following the written law had
no reason to prefer a type over a check, or a check over a sentence.

The family disposition of the study, treasury 0030, Take eight rules from
the skills study and refuse the rest, named the rulebook as this rule's home.
The two-tier rule lives in the guide's Hard rules, so the ladder joins it
there, beside the principle it completes.

## Evidence

A grep of every guide, rulebook, map, baseline, record, script and
configuration for the words impossible, unrepresentable, structural and by
construction found them used descriptively in records and never as a
preference about how a rule lands. Of the study's twenty-six candidates,
seven could land as configuration or as a check, and without a stated
preference every one would have been written as a sentence. The ladder's
source, a public skill collection built on the poka-yoke method, reports that
models asked to close a design named what it forecloses in 45 percent of
graded verdicts unprompted and 80 percent with the method, across 132 verdicts
and six model families.

## Options considered

- A sentence in the rulebook, as the disposition first named. Refused,
  because the rulebook governs documentation and prose and the two-tier rule
  it completes is in the guide.
- A gate item asking every change for its strongest form. Refused, because
  the gate binds a change and this binds the landing of a rule, which is the
  owner's act and happens once per rule.
- Leaving the preference practised and unstated. Refused, because the study
  showed the practice does not transfer by example, and a rule the model
  cannot read is a rule it follows only by luck.

## Decision

The guide's Hard rules gain one bullet immediately after the two-tier rule:
a rule lands in the strongest form its mistake allows, impossible by a type,
a refused import or a byte-identical copy where one can make it so, else a
check that decides and gates, else an advisory or a review clause, with prose
the last form. The form is chosen before the rule is written, and the record
of the rule names the form it took and why no stronger one was available.
The bullet is byte-identical in every guide, and the arrow carries it in the
guide's shared tail. This rule's own form is prose, because no check can
decide whether a stronger form existed for a rule not yet written.

## Consequences

The seven landings that follow from the same study take the form the ladder
picks, three of them as configuration or checks rather than sentences. Every
later record of a rule carries one more sentence, the rung it landed on and
why not a higher one, which is where a reviewer will first see a check that
should have been a type. No byte outside the guides changes.
