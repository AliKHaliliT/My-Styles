# 0112. Prefer a verb to the noun made from it

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-10-15

## Context

The prose law held that plain verbs carry claims best and named the
trailing participle and the "serves as" family, and it left the
commonest way a verb goes missing unnamed, the noun made from it, an
optimization performed, a measurement conducted, an analysis carried
out, each of which hides who acted and when behind a verb that says
nothing. A review method the owner keeps outside the family names it
in its language checklist, verbs instead of nominalizations where
agency matters.

## Options considered

- A grep for the "-tion of" shapes. Refused, because the shape is
  half of honest technical prose, and an advisory that fires on every
  "the evaluation of the model" teaches readers to dismiss it.

## Decision

A verb beats the noun made from it, "measured" over "performed a
measurement of", because the noun hides who did it and when. The
clause stands beside the plain-verbs sentence in the rulebook's Prose
section, one text across the family, held in review.

## Consequences

A sentence that reports an act names the act, and the actor comes
with it.
