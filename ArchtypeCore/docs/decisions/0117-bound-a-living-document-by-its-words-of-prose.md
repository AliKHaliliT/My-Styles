# 0117. Bound a living document by its words of prose

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-10-17

## Context

A living document was bounded at one hundred and fifty lines, a count
that measured wrapping and tables as much as prose, and the rulebook,
the guide, the map and the invariants ledger stood outside it because
they grow with the system. The guide and the rulebook were then split
into documents a reader takes in whole, and whole has a measure in
words, not lines; a document of two thousand words of prose is read in
one sitting, and the family's split documents all stand under it.

## Options considered

- Keep the line budget and widen it. Refused, because a line counts a
  table row and a wrapped sentence alike, so the number said nothing a
  reader feels.
- Bound every living document, the registers among them. Refused,
  because the state file, the invariants ledger and the bibliography
  grow by rows with what they register, and a register split by subject
  is a register nobody can search.

## Decision

A living document is bounded at two thousand words of prose, counted
outside its tables and fenced blocks, and the audit fails one past the
bound with fission as the remedy. A register, the state file and the
invariants ledger in every seat, with the questions file, the
bibliography and a child's upstream file in the host, grows by rows and
is measured by no budget. The README keeps the baseline's schema. The
guide, the map and the rulebook's documents, free-growing until now,
come under the bound, since each is now a document read whole.

## Consequences

Every prose document a reader is asked to read whole is one a reader can
read whole, and a document that grows past the bound splits at a seam.
