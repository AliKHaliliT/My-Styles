# 0138. Give a TLDR one shape for every answer

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-11-24

## Context

The style's owner asks for a TLDR often, and each one came back in a
different shape. The owner asked for one shape that holds for any kind
of answer, a question, an opinion, a finished or running task, a feature
under discussion or an upstream entry, very direct and to the point,
knowing that no list of kinds covers everything that will happen.

## Evidence

Read on 2026-11-24 at 0fdee3b. The rules of conduct held no rule on a
short summary. The draft was tried on six exchanges from the owner's own
sessions, one of each kind the owner named, and then read back twice
through a weaker model with situations no example covered, an
explanation that proposed nothing, a red gate with two options, an
external paper, a review with five findings, a refused fetch, a choice
among three libraries, an answer already two sentences long and a
summary of several messages. The first reading put a done line on the
explanation, could not tell whether a summary covers the last reply or a
whole discussion, and was unsure whether a short answer still takes
three lines. The rewritten rule came out as intended in every situation
of the second reading.

## Options considered

- Listing the kinds of answer with a shape for each. Refused, because no
  list covers every answer, and a rule that names each line's job holds
  for the kinds nobody listed.
- A cap in words. Refused, as a length rule was refused before, because
  it cuts filler and never information; three lines of one sentence each
  bound a summary by its form.
- Keeping the shape as one worker's habit. Refused, because a habit
  reaches one worker, while the rules of conduct reach every worker in
  every project built from the style.

## Decision

When the owner asks for a TLDR, the reply is three lines and nothing
else, each one sentence, even when the full answer is short. Bottom line
says what the full answer comes to, and after a task's closing note it
is that note's state line word for word. Why says what the bottom line
rests on, the source, the reason or the proof. Your move says what the
owner must do or decide next, by option number where options were posed,
or Nothing. The full answer is the reply the summary follows or the
stretch of discussion the owner names, and a summary adds no claim the
full answer did not make. No tool can read a reply, so the rule is held
in review.

## Consequences

Every TLDR in the family has the same three lines, so the owner finds
the verdict, its ground and the next move in the same place every time.
A situation nobody foresaw still has a shape, since each line is defined
by its job.
