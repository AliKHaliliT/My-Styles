# 0101. Quote a rule before explaining it

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-10-15

## Context

An agent asked what a rule means answers from memory, in its own
words, and the owner hears a rule that is close to the written one
and not it, and decides on the paraphrase. The family's law is bytes,
a style is copied whole and never summarised, a record is cited by
its title verbatim, and the gate re-reads the hard rules rather than
recalling them, and the one place the law was still being restated
was the conversation about it. A review method the owner keeps
outside the family answers every question about a term by quoting its
legend entry verbatim and only then adding an example.

## Options considered

- A legend of terms in every seat, quoted on demand. Refused, because
  the rulebook and the guide are the legend, and a third document
  restating them is the copy the rule exists to prevent.
- Nothing, trusting the reader to open the file. Refused, because the
  owner asked the agent, and the agent's paraphrase is what the owner
  gets.

## Decision

When the owner asks what a rule means, the agent quotes the rule's
own sentence from the guide or the rulebook before it explains it.
The sentence stands in every guide's reading bullet, held in review,
because a quotation and a paraphrase look alike to a tool.

## Consequences

An explanation of a rule begins with the rule, and a wrong reading is
visible against the bytes beside it.
